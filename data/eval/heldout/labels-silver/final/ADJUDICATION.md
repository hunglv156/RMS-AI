- Labeler: `adjudicated-v1.1`, date: 2026-11-22.
- Inputs: `data/eval/GUIDELINES.md` (v1 + section "v1.1 clarifications (2026-11-22)"), `jd-py-real`, `jd-hr-real`, `data/eval/heldout/set-heldout-10.yaml` → `data/eval/text/<sha>.txt`, and the two independent silver label sets in `data/eval/heldout/labels-silver/claude/` and `data/eval/heldout/labels-silver/gpt/` (unchanged).
- Method precedent: `data/eval/labels-silver/final/ADJUDICATION.md` (dev set). Its scope decisions were reused so that the dev and held-out finals follow the same label policy.
- Output: 20 files in this folder, same structure as the templates in `data/eval/heldout/labels/`.

## Method

1. Pairs were processed one at a time: read the full CV text, then the two prior labels side by side, then wrote and validated the final file before moving on.
2. The two prior labels were shown as "Annotator 1" and "Annotator 2" in a per-pair random order (seeded with the SHA-256 of the pair name), with labeler names and `overall_fit` hidden. The table below uses that order. **Limitation:** this masking did not fully hide the sources. One set writes its notes in Vietnamese and the other in English, and one source file was opened before the masked comparison started (while reading the file structure). Identities were not used as a reason for any decision, but the comparison cannot count as source-blind. See "Matches with each source".
3. Each criterion:
   - **[agree]**: both annotators chose the same level and at least one of their quotes supports it under v1 + v1.1, so that level was kept. Evidence = the supporting quotes, verbatim and deduplicated. Where one quote was a truncated or split copy of another, the full line was kept. Two quotes that did not support the criterion were dropped, and the note says so: database-h1__jd-py-real / postgresql_database (an Oracle query-tuning line) and hr-h3__jd-hr-real / hris_office_tools (claims systems AS400/Lotus Notes).
   - **[adjudicated]**: the level was re-judged from the CV text under v1 + v1.1. The final level may match either annotator or neither, and the note gives the reason. This covers (a) all 20 criteria where the annotators disagreed and (b) 15 criteria where both annotators chose the same level but that level conflicts with a v1.1 scope decision of the dev-set adjudication (listed below). Every other agreed level stood up under v1.1, including the rule-4 check on every agreed `strong`.
4. `overall_fit` was recomputed for all 20 pairs with rule 5 from the final levels of the `must_have` criteria. It is not a judgement call.
5. `expected_group` was not used. `data/eval/runs/`, `data/eval/labels/`, `data/eval/labels-silver/` (except the precedent ADJUDICATION.md), `apps/`, `.orca/`, `.lewis/` and `docs/` were not opened.

### How v1.1 was applied (scope decisions)

These are the same as the dev set unless marked *new*.

- **Rule 1 (experience)** applies to tenure-in-role criteria: `backend_experience` and `hr_experience`. If there is a dated history and no listed role is relevant → `absent`, with the role headings and dates as evidence. If a listed role is relevant but does not meet the requirement → `partial`, for example: about 6 years of PHP backend work against 7 required; 2 years as an agency Executive Recruiter against 3+ HR years; an HR role with no description. *New:* a pure Database Administrator role (installation, tuning, backup, monitoring) was not counted as backend software engineering, unlike the dev set's Programmer Analyst/DBA. Building a recruiting tool as an engineer was not counted as HR experience.
- **Rule 3 (skills/activities)** also covers the activity criteria `talent_acquisition`, `employee_relations_compliance` and `hr_programs`. If they are not mentioned → `unknown`, even when the dated history has no HR role. Both annotators applied rule 1 to `talent_acquisition` and `hr_programs` for the five non-HR CVs and chose `absent`. Those 10 agreed labels were adjudicated to `unknown`. Training team members is not counted as an HR program. *New:* engineering an internal recruiting tool is not recruitment (`talent_acquisition` stays `unknown`), but it is a technical contribution to an HR initiative (`hr_programs` = `partial`) and work with recruiting technology (`hris_office_tools` = `partial`).
- **Rule 2 (education)**: a stated degree in a non-matching field → `absent`. A degree in an adjacent field that partly matches → `partial`. For CS, that means Electronics & Communication Engineering (the dev precedent), and *by the same logic* Electrical Engineering and Computer Information Technology. For HR, that means an IT Management master's, a Technical Management degree, a business degree of unstated level, or an HR bachelor's that is only expected. Two agreed `absent` labels on `education_cs` (Electrical Engineering; ECE + IT Management) were adjudicated to `partial` on this basis. A related-field bachelor's (Business Administration, Finance) counts as `adequate` for the HR JD.
- **Rule 4 (`strong`)** was checked on every `strong` and on every disagreement involving `strong`. Tenure alone, a self-description or a list of duties → `adequate`. Numbers, scale or clear ownership (organization-wide communicator role, team lead of a billing project, 15 hires in 12 months, Top Producer awards, supervising 3 HR interns) → `strong`. A quantified result on only one part of a multi-part criterion (grant-writing revenue for `interpersonal_communication`, when confidential handling and internal communication are not shown) → `adequate`.
- **Communication criteria**: self-described only, generic, or client/sales communication → `partial`. Role-backed communication across roles, or explaining specialist or technical matters to non-specialists (managers, all employees, business users, end users) → `adequate`. `strong` only with concrete scale. For `communication_collaboration` on the Python JD, this means explaining HR policy to employees and managers, or billing errors to the business, is `adequate`. Three agreed `partial` labels were adjudicated to `adequate` on this basis (hr-h1, hr-h2, python_dev-h2).
- *New* — **multi-component criteria** (e.g. `cloud_docker_linux`: Linux, Bash, Docker, AWS): `adequate` when most named components are clearly and practically evidenced, `partial` when only a minority are, or when they appear only in skill or environment lists. The main relational-database criterion requires PostgreSQL itself: SQL depth on another engine with PostgreSQL only in a skills list → `partial`.
- OCR: python_dev-h3 has a systematic s/S → "Claire" substitution, but the text is readable. Quotes keep the OCR text verbatim, and `needs_review` was not needed.
